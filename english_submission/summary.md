# Campus Shuttle App Feedback Summary

## 1. One-Sentence Overview

Users value live shuttle information and notifications, but reliability, accessibility, safety, payment, privacy, and app-function concerns create significant barriers that require follow-up (R01–R12).

## 2. Top Themes

| Theme | Evidence IDs | User Impact | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unreliable status, arrival, and app behavior | R01, R04, R08, R09, R10, R11 | Users miss commitments, wait longer, see confusing status information, lose routes, or cannot use a paid pass. | Investigate each reported timing, frozen-tracker, language-change, status-label, and QR-loading case; verify behavior before selecting fixes. |
| Accessibility and readable access | R03, R06 | Low contrast limits outdoor readability, while an incorrect accessibility indication caused a rider to wait for another shuttle. | Review outdoor color contrast and verify that accessibility indicators match working shuttle features. |
| Requests for clearer choices and communication | R05, R07, R12 | Users lack preferred alert timing, want clarity about background location data, and may not want to install an app for delay notices. | Evaluate configurable notification timing, explain stored location data, and assess an SMS delay option. |
| Payment and pass fulfillment | R02, R11 | A duplicate charge needs review, and a paid visitor pass could not be displayed. | Review the duplicate-charge evidence and investigate paid-pass QR delivery, requesting missing transaction details as needed. |

## 3. Positive Feedback

- Morning estimates are usually acceptable for one user, although evening estimates are inaccurate (R01).
- The app is useful despite outdoor contrast problems (R03).
- A friendly driver helped a rider with a bag (R04).
- Nearby-shuttle notifications are appreciated (R05).
- Live buses and stop names improve on the old PDF schedule (R10).

## 4. High-Priority Risks

- An accessibility icon indicated an accessible shuttle, but the arriving shuttle had no working ramp, delaying the rider (R06).
- Late-night information showed “on time” while students waited outdoors and one user reported feeling unsafe after dark (R09).
- A paid visitor pass could not be used because its QR code never loaded on Wi-Fi or cellular (R11).
- A duplicate monthly-pass charge includes one pending and one posted charge; the refund request cannot yet be tied to a clear transaction ID (R02).

## 5. Missing or Ambiguous Information

- The affected routes, stops, device details, and app versions are not supplied for several timing and app-behavior reports (R01, R04, R08, R09, R10, R11).
- R08 suggests untranslated route names as a possible explanation, but the cause is not established.
- The screenshot is mentioned but its contents and the required transaction ID are not provided in the note (R02).
- The specific accessibility icon data source and the condition of the next shuttle are not stated (R06).
- The notes do not state what location data are stored or why background permission is requested (R07).

## 6. Recommended Next Steps

1. Prioritize case review for the accessibility mismatch, late-night safety concern, unusable paid QR pass, and duplicate charge (R06, R09, R11, R02).
2. Reproduce and measure the reported tracker, route, arrival-label, and evening-estimate behavior using the supplied times and language-change sequence (R01, R04, R08, R10).
3. Review contrast, alert timing, location-data communication, and SMS requests with accessibility, privacy, and product stakeholders (R03, R05, R07, R12).
